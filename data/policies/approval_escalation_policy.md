# Leave Approval and Escalation Policy (Version 1.2)

## 1. Approval Authority
### 1.1 Requests up to five working days
Leave requests of 5 working days or fewer are approved by the employee's reporting manager.
### 1.2 Requests longer than five working days
Leave requests longer than 5 working days must be approved by the skip-level manager (the reporting manager's own manager). If the employee's reporting manager has no manager, the reporting manager approves.
### 1.3 Loss of pay
Loss-of-pay requests additionally require HR Administrator approval.

## 2. When the Manager Is Unavailable
If the designated approver is inactive, has left the organisation, or is on approved leave on the day the request is submitted, the request is automatically routed to the next manager up the reporting chain. Employees do not need to resubmit.

## 3. Response Time and Escalation
Approvers should act on a request within 3 working days. Requests pending for more than 3 working days are flagged to the skip-level manager for follow-up. Requests that start within 2 working days should be prioritised.

## 4. Conflicts of Interest
No one may approve their own leave request. Approvers may only act on requests assigned to them. HR Administrators may view all requests but do not approve requests that are assigned to a manager.

## 5. Team Coverage
When reviewing requests, approvers should consider team coverage. As a guideline, at least 60% of a team should be available on any working day. Approvers should consider requests in order of start date, and should prioritise requests with sufficient balance and adequate notice.

## 6. Decisions
Every approval or rejection is recorded in the approval history with the approver, action and timestamp. A rejection should include a reason.
